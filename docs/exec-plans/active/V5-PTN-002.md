## Reconciliation against PR #24 (why it is not simply merged)

- PR #24's `resolvePartnerRoute` modeled eligibility on **capability admission alone** (`resolvePartnerCapabilityClaimStatus` against a `PartnerOrganization` + `PartnerCapabilityClaim` pair). Diffing PR #24's base (`912e66927e432c955fcd3135d302e0a8c941cc0c`) against current `main` confirmed `resolvePartnerCapabilityClaimStatus`'s signature/behavior is unchanged (only `revokePartnerCapabilityClaim`'s `revokedAt` validation was hardened in Rev62, unrelated to this function) — so PR #24's capability-side logic itself was not stale.
- What PR #24 never modeled is §6's own "explicit agency employee → client/account assignment is the authority" requirement (`partner-organization.ts`'s `PartnerClientAssignment`/`resolvePartnerClientAccess`, tenant/customer/project/serviceRef-scoped, per-employee). An admitted organization-level capability claim proves a partner organization **can** perform a class of work in general — it never proves any specific partner **employee** is actually authorized against the **specific** tenant/customer/project a routing decision is for. Routing on capability alone would let a capability-admitted partner organization be routed work for a client it has never been granted access to.
- This checkpoint therefore composes **both** existing authority dimensions into one routing decision — capability admission (`resolvePartnerCapabilityClaimStatus`) and per-employee client/project access (`resolvePartnerClientAccess`) — rather than porting PR #24 as-is or inventing a third, parallel authority model.

## Scope (this checkpoint)

- `src/domain/partner-routing-decision.ts`:
  - `PartnerRoutingCandidate` bundles one specific partner employee's contribution to a routing attempt: `partnerOrganization`, `capabilityClaim`, `partnerEmployeeMembership`, and that employee's own `clientAssignments`. This module has no persistence/lookup capability of its own (consistent with every other pure domain module in this codebase) — a caller supplies already-validated domain objects.
  - `resolvePartnerRoute(request)` — pure function, mirrors `resolveWorkerRoute`'s structure exactly. A candidate is eligible only when: its `capabilityClaim` and `partnerEmployeeMembership` structurally belong to its own `partnerOrganization`; the claim's `capabilityRef` matches `requiredCapabilityRef` and resolves `ADMITTED` as of the caller-supplied `asOf`; the candidate partner is not itself `decidedByOwnerId` (no self-routing, extending §12's "cannot self-certify" into routing); and the specific candidate employee resolves `AUTHORIZED` via `resolvePartnerClientAccess` for the request's `targetOwnership` (tenant/customer/project/serviceRef-scoped, revocation-aware). Selects the first eligible candidate in order; every other eligible candidate's partner organization (deduplicated) becomes `fallbackPartnerOrganizationIds`. Never throws on "no eligible candidate" — returns an explicit `REJECTED` decision with a mandatory `reason`. `reason` and `decidedByOwnerId` are always non-empty on every returned decision.
  - Exactly four sibling-module imports (`partner-organization.ts` × 2, `partner-capability-admission.ts`, `project-ownership.ts`), no filesystem/network/child_process coupling, no system-clock read, no persistence of the resulting decision.
- `tests/partner-routing-decision.test.ts` (23 tests: P1-P23) and `tests/v5-ptn-002-boundary-scan.test.ts` (6 tests) — the boundary scan pins the exact import list, the exported surface, and the "no secret/provider literal, no `Date.now()`" invariants.

## Architecture / semantic invariants (verified by test)

- An admitted capability claim alone is **not** sufficient to route work to a partner: a candidate with no client assignment at all for the target project (P17), only a **revoked** assignment (P18), or an assignment scoped to a **different** tenant/customer/project (P19) or a **different `serviceRef`** (P23, extending Rev62's own serviceRef-exactness invariant into routing) is rejected even though its capability claim is genuinely `ADMITTED`.
- Per-employee granularity is real, not cosmetic: two employees at the *same* partner organization, one authorized for the target project and one not, resolve differently (P22) — the authorized employee's membership id is recorded on the decision (P21), not merely the partner organization.
- All of PR #24's original semantics are preserved: capability-ref mismatch (P2), UNVERIFIED (P3)/EXPIRED (P4)/REVOKED (P5) claims rejected, self-routing excluded (P6), no-eligible-candidate REJECTED with empty fallback (P7), first-eligible-selected/rest-as-fallback-in-order (P8), structurally mismatched claim ownership treated as ineligible not thrown (P9), fail-closed on malformed structural input (P10-P13), `reason` always non-empty (P14), fallback deduplication (P15), an ineligible-but-earlier candidate never beating an eligible later one (P16), and a structurally mismatched employee-to-partner-org relationship treated as ineligible not thrown (P20).
