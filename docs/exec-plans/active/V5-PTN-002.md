## Goal

Provide a pure, side-effect-free routing decision over partner organizations for a required capability, that always records who decided the route (owner), why (reason), and which other eligible partners exist as fallback — extending §12's already-implemented capability-admission floor into the routing boundary itself, so that only a partner with a currently `ADMITTED` capability claim can ever be routed to.

## Scope (this checkpoint)

- `src/domain/partner-routing-decision.ts`:
  - `PartnerRoutingCandidate` — pairs a `PartnerOrganization` (type-only import from `partner-organization.ts`) with a `PartnerCapabilityClaim` (from `partner-capability-admission.ts`) for eligibility evaluation.
  - `resolvePartnerRoute(request)` — the only exported function. Filters `candidates` by: (a) structural link (`capabilityClaim.partnerOrganizationId === partnerOrganization.partnerOrganizationId`), (b) `capabilityClaim.capabilityRef === requiredCapabilityRef`, (c) `resolvePartnerCapabilityClaimStatus({claim, asOf}) === "ADMITTED"`, and (d) the candidate partner is not itself `decidedByOwnerId` (self-routing exclusion, extending §12's "cannot self-certify" discipline into routing). Returns `ROUTED` (selecting the first eligible candidate, recording every other eligible candidate as `fallbackPartnerOrganizationIds`, deduplicated) or `REJECTED` (no eligible candidate) — both carry a mandatory, non-empty `reason` and the mandatory `decidedByOwnerId`.

## Explicitly deferred (not invented)

No filesystem/network/child_process coupling, no persistence layer, no system-clock read (exactly two sibling-module imports, both from already-merged modules; every status resolution is bound to a caller-supplied `asOf`, never `Date.now()`). No capability catalog/taxonomy (mirrors `partner-capability-admission.ts`'s existing `capabilityRef` opaque-pointer convention). No commercial/legal/payment modeling (§12's "deliverable/evidence acceptance does not imply payment settlement" — untouched; nothing here settles or implies settlement of anything). No wiring into `resolvePartnerClientAccess` or any client-assignment gate — routing a capability request to a partner is a distinct concern from client/project access, and conflating them would be inventing scope. No persistence of the resulting `PartnerRoutingDecision` record anywhere — exactly like `resolveWorkerRoute`, this module computes a decision, it does not store one; a durable routing-decision log (if ever needed) is a distinct, larger persistence-layer concern intentionally left out of this bounded floor.

## Architecture / semantic invariants (verified by test)

- A candidate is eligible only when its claim structurally belongs to its own partner organization, matches the required capability, currently resolves `ADMITTED` as of the caller-supplied `asOf`, and its partner is not the routing decision's own owner.
- An `UNVERIFIED`, `EXPIRED` (computed via `resolvePartnerCapabilityClaimStatus`), or `REVOKED` claim can never make its candidate eligible, regardless of position in the candidate list.
- A structurally mismatched candidate (claim belongs to a different partner than declared) is excluded from eligibility rather than aborting the whole route — one corrupted candidate entry never blocks routing to the rest.
- `resolvePartnerRoute` never throws on "no eligible candidate" — it returns an explicit `REJECTED` decision with a reason, matching `resolveWorkerRoute`'s graceful-degradation discipline.
- Candidate order affects only which eligible candidate is selected first; an ineligible-but-earlier candidate is never chosen over an eligible later one.
- Every other eligible candidate besides the selected one is recorded, in order, deduplicated by id, as `fallbackPartnerOrganizationIds` — never fabricated, empty when none exists.
- `reason` and `decidedByOwnerId` are always non-empty strings on every returned decision, `ROUTED` or `REJECTED` alike.
